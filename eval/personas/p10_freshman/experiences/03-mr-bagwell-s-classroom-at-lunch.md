# Mr. Bagwell's classroom at lunch

_Junior and senior year of high school_

So there was this teacher, Mr. Bagwell. He did economics and he did the dual credit thing with HCC. Older white dude, kind of heavy, had a bad knee from like, I think he said he played JUCO football, and he had a Cowboys mug that said WORLD'S OKAYEST TEACHER, which, you know. Teacher humor.

And I ended up eating lunch in his room like almost every day junior and senior year. And it started for a dumb reason, it wasn't like a mentor thing, it started because the cafeteria was loud and I had gotten in the habit of, like — I would call home at lunch. Because that was when my mom was back from dropping Cami at her appointments some days, and I wanted to know how the labs came back. And you can't hear anything in the cafeteria. So I asked if I could sit in his room and he said 'don't get crumbs in the keyboard.' That's literally the whole conversation. That was it.

And so I'd be in there, and after like a week he just started giving me stuff to do. Like not in a mean way. He'd go, 'hey, if you're bored, those need to be collated.' And I'd collate them. Or he had this laminator and he was always laminating something, the man laminated things that did not need to be laminated, like he laminated a sign that said PLEASE PUSH IN CHAIRS. And I got good at the laminator. Like there's a technique, you gotta feed it straight or it gets a bubble.

And I think — okay this is the part. He never asked me anything. Like about my family or whatever. Two years. He knew I had a sister I guess because he'd hear me on the phone say her name, and one time he heard me say 'creatinina' or whatever and he didn't turn around. He just kept grading. And at the time I thought that was because he didn't care and then later I was like, oh. Maybe that was the thing he was doing. I don't know. I could be making that up.

And he wasn't like a big compliment guy either. Like, there was one time I did really good on a unit test, I think I got the highest, and he put it on my desk face down and said 'alright.' That's it. 'Alright.' And honestly that was better. Because when teachers do the thing where they go 'you're so bright, you're going places' — like the college readiness lady used to do that to me in the hallway, in front of people — I would get so, like, itchy. I'd just want to leave. I don't know why. She was being nice.

And then senior year he's the one who told me about the foundation scholarship. And the way he told me was he handed me a printed-out page, folded, and said 'the deadline's real.' And walked to the door to do hall duty. And that's it. And I did it.

The other thing about him, and this is kind of random, he ate the same lunch every day. Turkey sandwich, cut diagonal, and a bag of those pretzel things, and he'd give me the pretzels like half the time because he said his cardiologist was on him. And we didn't talk that much honestly. Sometimes we'd talk about the Texans. He hated the Texans, he was a Cowboys guy, that was like an ongoing bit.

And um. I never went back. Like after graduation. I keep meaning to email him, like I have literally composed the email, I have it in drafts, and I read it and it sounds so— it sounds like I want something. Or it sounds like I'm making it into a bigger deal than it was, like, dude, I laminated some signs in your room. So I haven't sent it. It's been like a year and three months. He probably has a whole new kid in there eating lunch.

I don't know why that's one of the things I'd say shaped me. It's not dramatic. Nothing happened. That's kind of the point I guess. Nothing happened for two years and it was the calmest I've been.
