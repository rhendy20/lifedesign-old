# The scholarship interview downtown

_March of senior year of high school, 2025_

Okay so, um. The interview thing. This was like March, senior year, and it was for the — I don't want to say the name but it's like a foundation, they do like twenty kids a year in Houston. And it was downtown in a hotel, not like a hotel room, it was one of those conference rooms off the lobby with the carpet that has the, like, swirls on it. I remember the carpet for some reason. And parking was twelve dollars but they validated it, they told us in the email they'd validate it and I still brought twelve dollars cash in my pocket just in case, because like, what if the guy at the booth doesn't take the ticket, you know? So I had it folded in my pocket the whole time.

My dad dropped me off, he didn't come in. He had to go to a job in Katy, he does like, he installs countertops, granite and quartz. And he was like, in the truck, he goes 'tú diles la verdad y ya,' like just tell them the truth and that's it. And I said okay. And then I get in there and I don't know, I didn't exactly lie but I— okay so they asked me the challenge question. Everybody knows they're gonna ask the challenge question. And I had it kind of, like, I had a version of it. About my sister. About her getting sick and stuff and how I helped out at home.

And the version I said was like — it was clean? Like it had a beginning and an end. Like 'she got diagnosed, it was hard, I learned time management.' Which, like. Time management. I said time management to three adults in a hotel. And the lady in the middle, she had these glasses on a chain, she was writing, and the guy on the left goes 'and how is she doing now?' And I said 'she's good, she's a sophomore, she's doing great.' Which she was, at that time, mostly. But like the week before that she'd had a thing where her ankles were swelling again and my mom was doing the thing where she doesn't sleep and she cleans the kitchen at like one in the morning. And I didn't say that. I don't know why I didn't say that. It wasn't like— it's not a lie. It's just I didn't want it to be, like, wet? Like I didn't want them looking at me a certain way.

Um. And then at the end the lady with the glasses said something like, 'you carry a lot for someone your age,' and I— I remember I went, 'oh, it's fine, the bus from my house is only like thirty-five minutes so it's not bad.' Which has nothing to do with anything. She was saying a nice thing and I started talking about the 65 bus. And then they kind of laughed and I laughed and that was the interview.

And then here's the part that I think about more than the actual interview. After, they were stacking the chairs, there was like a guy from the hotel doing it and there were maybe ten chairs, and I just started doing it with him. Like without thinking. And the lady with the glasses came out and saw me stacking chairs and she said 'oh you don't have to do that' and I said 'no it's okay' and I kept doing it. And I don't know. I felt way better doing that than I felt in the whole interview. Like my whole body was calm. In the interview my hands were doing this thing where I kept pressing them on my legs.

So. I got it. Four thousand a year, renewable, you have to keep a 3.0 and send a form in April. And when the email came I read it like four times and my first thought — I'm not exaggerating — my first thought was 'they're gonna find out something and take it back.' Like they're gonna realize my dad made more one year because he did a big job and we'll be over the number. I actually went and looked up the income cutoff again. Like at eleven at night. I have it saved.

My mom cried and called my tía in San Miguel on video and made me say the number out loud in Spanish, cuatro mil, and I said it and I was like, mami, it's per year, it's not all at once, it doesn't— and she was like 'ya, ya, díselo.' So I said it again. That's basically the whole thing. The carpet had swirls. I don't know why that stuck.
