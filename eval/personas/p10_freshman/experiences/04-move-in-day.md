# Move-in day

_Three weeks ago, August 2026_

So this is recent, this is like three weeks ago, um, move-in. And it was a hundred and one degrees. Like actually. And we came up in my dad's truck, the whole family, Cami came too which was a whole negotiation because of the heat and the sun stuff, she has to be careful with sun, so she had the hat and the long sleeves in August and she was so mad about it.

And the move-in thing, they have it super organized, they have like a lane and the volunteers in the orange shirts and the carts, and you get like a twenty minute window to unload. And we got there early, my dad had us leave at five thirty in the morning, and we were the third truck in the lane.

And okay so. My stuff was like — I had four things. Two of those big plaid zipper bags, a fan, and the plastic tub with the sheets. That's it. And so we're unloaded in like eight minutes. And then I just stood there. And there was a family next to us with, I'm not kidding, a whole entire Target, like a rug, a mini fridge, a futon, and the dad was doing the thing where he's mad at the mom about the futon. And I went over and grabbed the other end of the futon. And me and that dad carried it to the elevator. And my mom was like '¿qué haces?' and I was like 'nada, ya vengo.'

And my dad, he was walking around looking at the building, he kept touching stuff. Like he was looking at the door frames and the counters, like professionally. He does countertops so he was like inspecting the countertop in the lobby and he made a face. And he said the joints were bad. And that's how he said he was proud of me, I think. He said the joints were bad and then he put his hand on my neck for like a second.

And my mom made the bed. She brought the sheets from home, the ones with the— they're like, they're not nice sheets, they're the ones from home, and I had actually wanted to buy new ones with the dining hall money but she'd already washed those and folded them in the tub. And she made the bed and then she smoothed it like eight times. And she was crying a little but doing the thing where she pretends she's hot.

And then they had to go because of traffic and Cami needed to not be in the sun. And I walked them down and my dad gave me a hundred dollars, in twenties, which, like, I didn't want to take it and I took it, and I still have eighty of it because I'm not— I don't know, I keep it in the tub.

And then the truck left. And I went back up and I sat on that bed my mom made, and honestly? For like the first ten minutes I was so relieved. Like physically. Like something let go in my chest and then immediately I was like, what is wrong with you. Your mom just cried in a parking lot. And then I got up and unpacked, like, immediately, in like twenty minutes, because I didn't want to sit there.

And then I texted them a picture out the window and I said 'buena vista' — good view. And the view is a parking garage. It's a parking garage and like a piece of a tree. And I don't know why I said that. I do it constantly. I did it the first week too, like my roommate, he's from Plano, nice guy, he's got the full-size fridge and he talks to his mom on speaker in the room like she's just there, and the first week I was kind of — not homesick exactly, it was more like I didn't know what I was for? Like at home there's stuff to do. There's always stuff. And here it's just, go to class, and go to work at the dining hall, and then it's four in the afternoon and nobody needs anything.

So I call Sunday at seven. And the first thing I ask, every time, before anything, is how were Cami's numbers. And my mom's started going 'hola primero, Elmer.' Like, say hi first. And I'm like, hi. How were her numbers.
