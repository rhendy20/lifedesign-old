# The application in the door pocket

_2011, age 37_

So, the promotion thing. Everybody in my family brings this up so I might as well. In 2011 Marlene's job came open — well, it wasn't Marlene's anymore by then, it was Supervisor Two, Billing and Customer Accounts, after they did the reclass. And I want to be honest, everybody in that office including the girls up front assumed it was mine. Doreen literally brought me a card. She brought me a congratulations card before I applied. Which, I think about that too. And I didn't put in. And here's the thing, everybody thinks there's a reason, like some big — there's not. Okay. So HR puts the posting up, I think it was a Thursday, and I took the packet home and it sat in the door pocket of my truck. The driver's door, where you put your ice scraper. Eleven days. And I filled out everything. Name, dates of service, my certs, I got my Ohio Water Environment certification in '02, all of it, I filled it out in pen at the kitchen table. There's one section, it's Section Six, and it says 'Describe your supervisory philosophy and give an example of a time you addressed a performance issue.' Half a page, lines on it. And I never wrote in it. Eleven days. And the deadline's noon on a Friday and I drove past the admin building on Sandusky Street on my lunch and I just — I kept going. I went to Arby's. That's it. That's the whole dramatic story. Arby's. And what I told Tammy that night, and this is true, this is all factually true, is the money was four thousand two hundred a year before taxes, which after the PERS pickup and going up a bracket is like, what, two hundred bucks a month, and you lose the overtime eligibility, and Nathan was doing travel ball that summer, they were going to Cooperstown, and I'd have been on call for main breaks. Which is all true. Every bit of that is true. And she said okay. She didn't push. Tammy's — she's smarter than me and she knew, I think she knew not to push. So Brad Nusser got it. Thirty-one years old, came over from the engineering side, nice enough kid, tucks his shirt in. And I trained him. I mean I trained him on everything, I sat with him for, God, six weeks, and he's my boss for now fifteen years and he's never once had to look at me sideways and I've never had to — you know. We're good. We're genuinely good. He brings me a Dairy Queen cake on my anniversary date every year, which is a whole thing, everybody laughs about it. And I don't regret it. I want that on the record, I do not regret it. I sleep like a rock. Because what happened was in, ah, I want to say 2014 or '15, Brad had to do a corrective action on Cheryl Vandersteen, who'd been there eighteen years, over her time card stuff, and they needed a witness from the department and it was me. And that was a Tuesday. It was forty minutes in the little conference room with the whiteboard nobody erases. Forty minutes. And Cheryl cried, and then afterward she went back out to her desk and did her whole afternoon, answered her phones and everything, and I'm two rows over. That's — anyway, unrelated, I'm just saying it's not all gravy, that job. It's not just the four thousand. Oh, here's the stupid part. I still got the application. It's in the gray filing cabinet in the garage, second drawer, in with the manuals for the snowblower and stuff. Tammy found it a couple years back when we were doing the garage and she held it up and she didn't say anything, she just looked at me, and I said throw it out, and she put it back in the drawer. So it's still in there. I don't know why I didn't throw it out. I keep everything, that's probably it. I got receipts in there from the Buick and we sold the Buick in 2009.
