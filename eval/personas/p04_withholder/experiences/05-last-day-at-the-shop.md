# Last day at the shop

_March 2026_

So I'm retired since March. Twenty-six. Uh, this March, yeah.

The way it went, there was talk. There's always talk, but this was the kind where the plant manager's walking around with a guy nobody knows and the guy's got a tablet. So I went down to HR on a Monday and I took the package before they put the list out. Two weeks later the list come out and there was fourteen on it. I don't know if I was on it. Wasn't gonna find out.

Last day was a Thursday. I got in at six like always, cleaned the box out, ran the drawers with a rag, 'cause you leave a box dirty and that's what they remember. Took the Starrett, took my dial indicator, the 0-1 test indicator with the swivel, took the tap wrenches that were mine. Left the rest. There's probably three hundred dollars of my own tooling in that shop still, which, fine.

There was a card going around and a cake for two o'clock. I left at eleven-forty. At lunch. Went out the side by the compressor room and put the box in the truck and that was it. Forty-one years, if you count the fittings place, thirty-four at this one.

There's a kid there, Devon, twenty-three, twenty-four. Good hands, terrible with a print. His mic set was junk, chinese stuff, the ratchet on the two-inch was, it wasn't repeatable, you'd get four different readings. So I got him a used Mitutoyo set off a guy in Girard, hundred and ninety, and I put it in his drawer and told him the company had 'em sitting in the crib from the layoff. He don't know. It's fine, he uses 'em, that's the point.

What I do now is, uh. I get up the same time. I go to the Denny's on Peach or the one out by 90. Then I don't know.

I built Nick a rod. Fly rod, six weight, cork grip I turned myself, wraps in green 'cause Cascade green was his hockey colors when he was little. Finished it in, uh, June. It's in the closet in the spare room in the tube.

He's in at Christmas. I'll see.

Anyway that's the — that's it, that's the story. Not much of one.
