# Moving out to Zuck Road

_2003, the divorce_

Okay so, the — uh. Deb and I. Twenty-oh-three.

She didn't ask me to go. I want to be fair on that. She never said the word. She was, uh, she was at her mother's a lot that spring and I could see which way it was pointed, so I got a place on Zuck Road, the brick ones behind the Wendy's, four ninety-five a month plus the electric. Signed the lease on a Tuesday, moved Saturday. Took the toolbox, the chest freezer I bought, and the dog. Duke. She didn't want the dog, the dog barked.

Took two loads with the truck. The box you gotta take the drawers out or you'll bend the frame, and it's still — with the drawers out it's four hundred and some pounds, so me and a guy from work, Timmy Pahl, we had it on a furniture dolly and a ramp.

Nick was twelve. He was at a thing, a sleepover or a — yeah.

The lawyer was on Peach, up by the courthouse, and it was, uh, it was not a fight. We split it and that was it. She kept the house, which, fine, the house was hers, her dad put the roof on it.

The furnace went out on her that January. Old Carrier, it's the flame sensor nine times out of ten, they get a film on 'em and you take 'em out and hit 'em with a Scotch-Brite pad and they're good another five years. So I went over on a Sunday and did it. Twenty minutes. Left before she got back from church. Put the snow tires on her Lumina the year after that too, that was early, like five-thirty, 'cause I didn't want to — well, it's better to do it before the salt trucks are out.

She had a knee done, I think, uh, four or five years ago. The left one. She's alright.

Christmas Eve that first year I sat out front of the house for a while in the truck before I went in to get him. That's — I don't know why I brought that up. Radio was on.

Anyway. Duke lived till oh-nine.
