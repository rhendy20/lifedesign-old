# Leaving to start my own thing

_Age 30_

So people ask about this a lot, like, the jump. The founder jump. And I've got a version I say at like, you know, if I'm on a panel at some Silicon Slopes thing, I say "I saw a problem nobody was solving for the small contractor, the two-truck guy, and I couldn't not build it." And that's — that's like eighty percent true.

Here's the actual timeline. September, they hired a VP of Revenue. Marcus. From, uh, from a bigger company in Austin. Really smart guy, genuinely, like I learned stuff from him in three months. And he came in above me and above sales, and he had his own way of doing things, he was very, um, he was a process guy. Big on forecast calls. Two a week. Wednesday and Friday. And you'd go through your number in front of everybody and he'd ask you three questions about every deal and they were good questions, that's the thing, they were good questions.

And I just — I felt sick on Wednesdays. Like physically. I'd be in the parking lot at 8:50 not wanting to go in. And I've never had that in my life. Never once.

So October I start building at night. Nights and Saturdays. I hired a contractor in Brazil actually, which was fun, I got to use my Portuguese, guy named Rodrigo, and we built this dispatch thing for the really small guys, like one to five trucks, because our product at the startup had gotten too heavy for them, we kept pricing them out. Which is a real problem! It was a real problem. I'd been saying it for two years in meetings and nobody moved on it.

And I gave notice in February. And, uh, three weeks later they announced a reorg where CS and onboarding merged and there was a new role, VP of Customer, and — I mean, whatever. I probably would've been in the mix. Maybe. There were like two other people who probably would've been in the mix too. Honestly it might've gone to Priya. It probably would've gone to Priya. So.

One thing I remember. They did a leadership offsite in Park City in January and I wasn't on the invite list. And at the time I told Britt it was because they were only doing VP and up and I was a director. And that's, that is true, that was the criteria. But two directors went. So.

But also I wouldn't have gone. Like I had my thing going at night by then, and honestly a weekend in Park City doing trust falls with Marcus? No thank you. I would've found a way out of it. I genuinely would have.

The weekend I decided, I cleaned the garage. Like, the whole thing. Twelve hours, both days. Pegboard, labeled bins, I swept the ceiling, who sweeps a ceiling. Britt came out Sunday afternoon and just stood there and goes, "So we're leaving the job, huh." Because she knows. She knows what the garage means.

And the thing is — we're four and a half years in now. We did 1.4 last year, nine people, it's real, it's profitable, I take a salary that's honestly less than I was making at the startup, which, Britt has thoughts about that. But I own a hundred percent of it. A hundred. We had a seed guy come at us in year two, decent terms, would've been like 1.2 on a — whatever, it was fine terms, and part of the deal was a board seat and I just, I couldn't get there. I told everybody it was dilution, and yeah, dilution, sure. But I sat in my car in the parking lot of the Kneaders in Lehi after that meeting for like forty minutes and what I kept thinking was, there'd be a guy. There'd be a guy I'd have to call every quarter and explain myself to.

And we'd probably be at four or five million right now if I'd taken it. Like, probably. My buddy Sam took money and he's at eleven. Eleven!

But I sleep. That's the trade. I sleep.
